# Which theory? The data says: both, on different margins

**Date:** 2026-08-28
**Question:** anchor the paper on real options / irreversibility (anchor 1) or institutional
voids / financial development (anchor 2)?
**Answer from the evidence:** they explain different features and the paper should use both,
layered — anchor 1 for the *response* (why the shock moved no investment), anchor 2 for the
*level* (why baseline investment is thin and why the durable opportunities the shock revealed
go unfunded). The two literatures are usually run separately; the case shows they are
complementary, and that is the theoretical contribution.

## Anchor 1 (real options) explains the shock-specific null

**Test D — captive state capital also abstained.** QIC/Baiterek faces no exit or
external-finance constraint. Of its 2022--2025 project pipeline (50 industrial/logistics
projects): **1** in a surge-basket sector (\$4m of QIC money), **2** in corridor logistics;
the other **47** are domestic-market agri-processing, steel (the Qarmet rescue), chemicals and
energy. The actor that *cannot* be blocked by institutional voids put essentially nothing into
the reorientation. The shock-specific null is therefore not a financing story.

**Test F — durable vs transitory shock, institutions held fixed.** In the same country, same
period, same (weak) financial market: the *durable* demand shock (autos --- Western brands
exiting Russia, the Kazakh consumer boom, EAEU market access) produced **real new capacity**
--- Chinese-brand SKD plants (\~90k units), a KIA plant (\$200m), \v{S}koda assembly, a
car-multimedia plant (Almaty, 2024). The *transitory* component shock produced **zero plants**.
Persistence flips the sign with institutions constant.

**Test C --- the "transitory" condition is observed, not assumed.** The surge-basket
KZ$\to$Russia flow peaked in September 2022 and then declined \~30\% over 2023-H2 (\$21m/month
in 2023-H1 $\to$ \$15m/month in 2023-H2) as enforcement tightened (EU 11th package and its
anti-circumvention tool, June 2023; stepped-up US/EU pressure on Kazakhstan through 2023). A
firm watching in real time saw the flow already receding within 18 months --- the exact
environment in which irreversible capital is not committed.

**The unit-value wedge is the model's key parameter.** Pass-through slope 0.27, median
re-export/import unit-value ratio 0.75: the production/transformation margin is not even
positive relative to the pure-trade margin. In a threshold model of the potential entrant,
investment is dominated before the option value of waiting is even added.

## Anchor 2 (institutional voids) explains the level and the residual

- **Baseline manufacturing investment is chronically thin** --- 7 deals/year in
  manufacturing + transport + distribution across 2015--2025, flat. A pre-existing condition,
  not caused by the shock.
- **The durable, low-sunk-cost opportunities the shock revealed go unfunded by private
  capital.** Warehousing / transport support has a domestic value-added multiplier of 0.82 and
  is the corridor's binding constraint; machinery and fabricated-metal import substitution
  have multipliers \~0.80 and import/output ratios of 2--8. These are financed, slowly, only
  by directed state capital --- there is no private growth capital and no exit (buy-back is
  the leading PE exit route).
- **Cross-country (weak, collinear).** The five reorientation intermediaries (Armenia,
  Georgia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyz Republic, Uzbekistan) all cluster at low financial development
  (private credit + market capitalisation 24--66\% of GDP, all below the comparator median).
  Shock type and financial depth are collinear in this sample, so country-level macro
  aggregates (FDI/GDP, gross fixed capital formation, manufacturing value-added growth) do not
  separate the two mechanisms --- the within-country tests do. A proper separation needs
  greenfield-project data by sector and country (fDi Markets / Dealroom), which is the natural
  extension.

## The framework for the paper

The investment response to a positive, sector-specific tradeable-demand shock is
$$ R \;=\; \underbrace{f(\text{persistence},\ 1-\text{policy uncertainty},\ 1/\text{sunk-cost intensity})}_{\text{real-options gate --- determines whether anyone builds}} \times \underbrace{g(\text{financial depth},\ \text{exit availability},\ \text{private/state capital mix})}_{\text{institutional gate --- determines whether the viable projects get funded}} \times \underbrace{h(\text{need to transform to capture the demand})}_{\text{market-access gate}} . $$

Kazakhstan: the real-options gate is near-closed (transitory, policy-contingent, high sunk
cost) so $R \approx 0$ regardless of the other terms; the market-access gate is also open
(EAEU $\Rightarrow$ no transformation needed to serve Russia), which is why the supply
response that *did* occur was trade, not production. The institutional gate then explains why
the durable adjacent opportunities (logistics, machinery substitution) --- for which the
real-options gate is open --- still are not being financed.

## Moderators (testable, organise the cross-country discussion)

| Moderator | Gate | Kazakhstan | Sign of $R$ when favourable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shock persistence (structural vs sanctions-driven) | real options | unfavourable (transitory) | + |
| Policy / enforcement uncertainty | real options | unfavourable (high) | + when low |
| Sunk-cost intensity of the technology | real options | unfavourable (component mfg) | + when low |
| Need to transform to capture the demand (rules of origin, local content, no internal border) | market access | unfavourable (EAEU, none) | + when required |
| Financial depth / exit availability | institutional | unfavourable | + |
| Private vs state capital mix | institutional | unfavourable (state-dominated, directed) | + when private-led |
| Existing productive base / absorptive capacity | institutional / increasing returns | unfavourable (electronics output \~\$230m) | + when a base exists |

External-validity reading: Vietnam / Mexico / Poland got shocks perceived as **structural**
(favourable persistence gate) and, for autos, tightened **rules of origin** (favourable
market-access gate) --- and did invest in capacity. Armenia and the Kyrgyz Republic share
Kazakhstan's configuration on every gate --- and show the same null.

## Recommendation

Write the Framework section around the multiplicative-gate structure above, with real options
as the lead concept (it is what closes $R$ to zero here) and institutional voids as the second
gate (it is what keeps the durable alternative unfunded). Target journal unchanged by this ---
JIE / JDE / EER all take an investment-under-uncertainty framing with an institutional second
stage. If a reviewer wants the cross-country separation made rigorous, that is the greenfield
-FDI-by-sector panel, flagged as the extension.
