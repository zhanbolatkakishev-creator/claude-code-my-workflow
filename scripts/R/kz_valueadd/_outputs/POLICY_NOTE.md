# Raising domestic value capture from trade through Kazakhstan — a policy note

*Companion to "Passing Through" and the value-add analysis. Grounded in: the trade-reorientation
result (retained value added ~8--10% of the gross flow, no markup, transit-only); the deal data
(no investment build-out in the relevant sectors); the Kazakhstan PE-market evidence
(institutional voids --- no exit market, state-dominated capital, informal sourcing); and
Kazakhstan's own 2X trade-strategy diagnostics.*

## Framing

Two distinct objectives, often conflated:

1. **Capture more from the flows that already exist** --- the transit and forwarding business.
   Low ambition, low risk, quick.
2. **Convert some of that flow into genuine processing / assembly** --- higher domestic value
   added. High ambition, only viable where Kazakhstan has a real cost or logistics advantage,
   and not on the back of a flow that is temporary and carries secondary-sanctions risk.

The evidence says Kazakhstan is currently getting little of (1) and none of (2). The
recommendations below are ordered by ratio of payoff to effort.

---

## A. Capture more of the transit margin (do first)

**A1. Price the corridor, don't just move it.** The retained margin is thin partly because
Kazakhstan competes on being the path of least resistance rather than on a priced service.
Introduce a single, published Middle-Corridor tariff with service-level guarantees (transit
time, loss rate), negotiated with the other corridor states, so the forwarding business is a
priced product with a margin rather than a residual.

**A2. Close the under-invoicing leak.** The outbound-leg unit value runs *below* the inbound
unit value (pass-through slope 0.27). That is lost tax base and lost visibility. Mandatory
electronic transit declarations reconciled against the partner's export record (mirror check
at HS6), with a risk-scored audit of cells where the outbound/inbound unit-value ratio is
implausible. This raises measured value added and duty without touching the flow.

**A3. Make customs clearance a revenue event, not a leakage.** Where goods are formally
imported (not bonded transit), ensure the EAEU common external tariff and fees are actually
assessed on a defensible customs value; where they transit, ensure the bonded regime is
properly bonded (guarantees, tracking) so it is not a duty-free back door.

**A4. Sell the ancillary services.** Trade finance licensing for forwarders, a cargo-insurance
regulatory sandbox, bonded warehousing with value-added-logistics (labelling, kitting,
quality control, returns processing) --- these are low-capex ways to move from "path" to
"platform" and they are already in Kazakhstan's own 2X plan. Prioritise them; they need
regulation, not subsidy.

## B. Convert transit into processing --- selectively

**B1. Do not chase re-export rents with factories.** The flow that surged is temporary,
sanctions-linked and thin-margin. Building electronics or component assembly to serve it would
be a stranded asset when enforcement tightens (as it already is). Industrial policy should
target products where Kazakhstan has a durable advantage --- energy-intensive materials
processing, agri-processing, some machinery for the domestic and Central Asian market --- not
the surge basket.

**B2. Where assembly does happen, require *real* local content, phased.** Kazakhstan's existing
car "assembly" is SKD kit assembly with low domestic value added --- the corridor logic one
step in. New incentive agreements should tie benefits to a rising, audited domestic-value-added
percentage (parts localisation, local engineering, not just final screwdriver assembly), with
claw-backs. Rules of origin should be enforced so "made in Kazakhstan" reflects substantial
transformation.

**B3. Use the special economic zones for what they are good at.** Airport free zones and the
Almaty logistics hub can host regional distribution, light assembly and after-sales for
higher-value goods aimed at the Central Asian market --- a genuine niche --- rather than being
generic tax holidays. Condition SEZ residency on employment and local-content targets.

## C. Fix the investment-climate constraints (necessary for any of B to work)

The deal data show chronic under-investment in tradeable manufacturing and a pipeline crowded
by nationalisations; the PE survey shows why capital does not flow to growth-stage industrial
firms.

**C1. Build an exit market.** Buy-back is the leading PE exit route in Kazakhstan --- a symptom
of no trade-sale or IPO market. Predictable minority-shareholder protection, a functioning
listing venue (AIX) with real free float, and a state posture of *selling* rather than
*acquiring* assets would let private growth capital enter industrial firms with a credible exit.

**C2. Crowd in, don't crowd out.** State and DFI capital dominate; private growth equity is
almost absent. Re-orient QIC / Baiterek toward co-investment *alongside* private funds
(matching, first-loss, or fund-of-funds) with private lead investors and market-terms
governance, rather than as the dominant direct investor.

**C3. Stop absorbing the pipeline with nationalisations.** Qarmet, Eurocopter and Lokomotiv
took state balance-sheet capacity and management attention in 2023--24. A clear
re-privatisation timetable for these assets frees resources and signals policy direction.

**C4. Skills and the boring inputs.** Component assembly needs technicians, testing labs,
metrology and certification infrastructure, and reliable industrial power and water. These are
the constraints that a tax incentive cannot fix; fund them directly.

## D. What not to do

- **Do not offer bespoke incentives to attract re-export intermediation.** It adds no domestic
  value, it invites secondary-sanctions exposure, and the flow leaves when enforcement
  tightens.
- **Do not target the surge-basket products for localisation.** They are the wrong products
  for Kazakhstan's factor endowment and the wrong flow to bet capital on.
- **Do not treat rising gross-trade numbers as success.** They are a corridor statistic. The
  metric that matters is domestic value added per unit of trade --- and that is what this work
  measures.

---

## One-line version

Kazakhstan should tax and price the transit business it already has (A), invest in genuine
processing only where it has a durable advantage and only with audited local-content
conditions (B), and fix the exit market and the state-vs-private balance in industrial finance
so that private capital can build that capacity (C) --- while explicitly not chasing the
re-export flow with factories or incentives (D).
