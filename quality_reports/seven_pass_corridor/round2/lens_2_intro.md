## Rubric walk-through

### Opens on the phenomenon, not a lit tour? — PASS

Para 1 (line 69–75) is the prior, not a citation list: "When demand for a country's tradeable
output rises sharply and persistently, the textbook expectation is that supply follows: firms
expand, new entrants appear …". Para 2 (line 77–86) is the phenomenon: "After February 2022
Kazakhstan received an unusually clean version of such a signal." No lit tour. The only quibble
is that para 1 carries **zero citations** — "modern work on global-value-chain entry and the
investment effects of trade shocks" is an un-anchored gesture toward the very literature the
paper engages (cited later at line 145). One anchor cite in para 1 would not hurt. MINOR at
most; the hook-first choice is legitimate.

### Hook → context → what this paper does → contribution → roadmap, in order? — PASS with one imbalance

Order is: prior (¶1) → phenomenon + Chupilkin (¶2) → "This paper asks what Kazakhstan did with
that signal. We proceed in three steps." (line 88) → three `\emph{}` steps (line 90–119) →
**framework paragraph** (line 121–137) → `\paragraph{Contribution}` i/ii/iii (line 139–154) →
roadmap (line 156–162). Canonical, except the framework paragraph is an **18-line theory block
wedged between the three steps and the contribution** — see MAJOR 3.

### Chupilkin citations placed to locate the gap? — PARTIAL (MINOR 1)

Both Chupilkin cites are in ¶2 (line 79–82): "\citet{chupilkin2026roundabout} document the
pattern … \citet{chupilkin2025intermediated} quantify the substitution." They do
phenomenon-documentation work. The **gap relative to them** — "extending the existing
literature — which establishes that rerouting happened and its scale — to what the intermediary
retains, and to 2025" — appears only at line 141–143, ~55 lines and one section-worth of
material later, buried in Contribution (i). ¶2 ends on "The routes, the demand and the products
are all observable in the trade data" (line 85–86), which bridges to "measure the shock" but
tells the reader nothing about what Chupilkin *left undone*. A one-line gap statement belongs
at the end of ¶2, adjacent to the citation.

### Contribution delineated i/ii/iii, leading with the empirical result? — YES

(i) is explicitly "Its primary contribution is empirical" and leads with the incidence analysis
+ the null (line 140–145). (ii) is the decomposition, now disclaimed. (iii) is the multi-source
deal-data method. Round-1 M1 is resolved. Residual wording problem in (ii): line 150 says the
two comparisons "argue **the irreversibility gate is the binding one** here" — but §7's own
finding (line 634–641) is that "The market-access gate is plainly open … That alone is close to
sufficient for R ≈ 0," with irreversibility "independently closed **as well**." Calling
irreversibility "the binding one" when §7 says market-access is close to sufficient on its own
is an imprecise compression. MINOR (folds into MAJOR 1).

### Does §1 still promise more than §2–§10 deliver? — YES, on the hedges (MAJOR 1, MAJOR 2)

No *phantom* promises — every method §1 previews (randomisation inference, Armenia/Kyrgyz
parallel, I-O propagation, three-database cross-check, QIC register, the §9 "where investment
would pay" analysis) exists in the body. The problem is **tone at the point of first
statement**:

- **"Which gate binds" / within-country comparisons.** §1 line 131–137: "Two within-country
  comparisons **then point to the irreversibility gate as independently binding**: the state
  investment corporation … also did not invest in the reorientation, and **the same
  institutions in the same years produced real capacity for a *durable* demand shock (vehicle
  assembly) while producing none for the transitory one.**" This is stated flat, as a clean
  within-Kazakhstan natural experiment. §7 spends three paragraphs retracting exactly that
  reading: line 692–701, "We treat this as **illustrative rather than as a controlled test**,
  for three reasons. In the deal databases, auto-sector transactions actually *fell* after
  2022 … the capacity evidence is from **plant announcements and public reporting, not from
  the deal data**, and **no symmetric announcement search was run** for the component sector.
  §4 notes that much of the auto capacity **predates** the reorientation. And the two shocks
  differ not only in persistence ρ but in sunk-cost intensity, in whether market access
  requires domestic content … and in the destination market. … it is **not a clean ρ
  experiment.**" And on QIC, §7 line 650–656: "**Two caveats keep it from being decisive.**"
  None of "illustrative," "not decisive," "not a clean identification," "suggestive," "much of
  the auto capacity predates the reorientation" appears anywhere in §1. The only disclaimer §1
  carries — line 151, "The decomposition is an organizing device rather than an estimated
  model" — is 14–20 lines downstream, in a different paragraph, and attaches to *the
  decomposition*, not to *the two empirical comparisons*. Import one hedging clause into §1
  where the comparisons are previewed.

- **The investment null presented as cleanly attributable.** §1 Step three opens (line 109):
  "*Third, we ask whether the shock induced investment* — and **find that it did not.**" Flat.
  Contribution (i) (line 143–145): "a large, **well-identified**, sector-specific
  tradeable-demand shock produced no detectable investment response." Yet §2 (line 186–191)
  concedes: "Three further shocks bear specifically on the *investment* null and **cannot be
  differenced away**: the January 2022 unrest … the secondary-sanctions and compliance
  exposure … and the 2022–2025 wave of nationalisations … they make the investment null
  *over*-determined rather than fragile, but **they also mean we cannot cleanly attribute it
  to the trade shock alone.**" §1 gives the reader none of this. It also drops §6's power
  caveat — §6 line 560–561, "with only four post-2022 years, the deal-count test can **rule
  out a large response, not a modest one**" — from Step three; the qualifier word
  "detectable" survives only in the Contribution paragraph (line 143). One concessive clause
  in Step three ("though three contemporaneous 2022 shocks also depress entry, so we bound the
  null's magnitude rather than attribute it") is needed.

### §1 confound paragraph — belongs in the intro, or too much detail? — There is NO confound paragraph in §1 (MAJOR 2)

Point of fact: the Qandy Qantar / secondary-sanctions / nationalisations paragraph is in **§2
(Setting), line 176–191**, not §1. For §2 the placement is right and the detail level is
acceptable (it does real work separating the reorientation from "New Kazakhstan" and flagging
the three non-differenceable shocks); trim 2–3 lines if anything. The defect is the mirror
image of the question as posed: **§1 has none of it**, and §1's Step-three + Contribution (i)
over-clean the null as a result (see MAJOR 2). Fix is in §1, not §2 — one clause, not the whole
paragraph.

### Replication-vs-novel boundary for Step 1 — BLURRY (MINOR 2)

¶2 credits Chupilkin with "document the pattern" and "quantify the substitution" (line 81–82).
Step one then says "*First, we measure the shock*" (line 90) and produces a 29-line basket,
Bai–Perron breaks, an event-study profile and a DiD — several of which (the aggregate
substitution magnitude, the 2022 timing) substantially **re-establish** Chupilkin at finer
resolution rather than discover something new. The abstract (line 38–43) states the shock
measurement as the paper's own with no "consistent with Chupilkin" nod, and §4 never
reconnects to ¶2's citation. The novel content is steps 2–3 (retention + the investment null);
Contribution (i) says as much ("extending … to what the intermediary retains, and to 2025"),
but the intro would be cleaner with an explicit line at Step one: "Chupilkin et al. establish
the aggregate substitution; we resolve it to HS6, date it to the month, and carry it to 2025
as the input to the incidence question." As written, a referee could read §4 as claiming
originality for a confirmation.

### de Souza et al. dynamic-gains point — integrated or bolted on? — BOLTED ON, and absent from §1 (MINOR 4)

`\citet{desouza2026diffusion}` appears in §3 (line 242–250) and §10 (line 872–876). It is
**not in §1 or the abstract at all**. §3's use is a 9-line detour into Brazilian firm-to-firm
technology-transfer records in the middle of the gate taxonomy — logically attached to the
market-access gate, but it reads as an inserted paragraph. §10 then **elevates** it: line
872–876, "the static value that is missed is the smaller loss: an open market-access gate also
forgoes the technology transfer and learning … **which is where the long-run cost of being a
corridor sits.**" That is close to the paper's welfare punchline, and §1 never gestures at it
— §1 previews the static 5–9% figure prominently (line 104–107) but says nothing about a
dynamic/learning loss. Either §10's "where the long-run cost sits" is over-stated, or §1
under-previews it. If §10 keeps that framing, this rises to MAJOR; as a one-sentence thread it
is MINOR for now. Recommend either a half-clause in §1's framework paragraph ("… and, because
a pure re-export gives the foreign supplier no reason to transfer technology, forgoes the
learning a plant would bring") or a softening of the §10 sentence.

### Roadmap (line 156–162) — MINOR 5

One ~90-word sentence covering ten section pointers ("Section~\ref{sec:setting} describes the
setting; Section~\ref{sec:framework} sets out the framework; …"). Standard format, not wrong,
but round-1 M13 flagged the abstract's nine-clause roadmap sentence; the same habit is here.
Break into two sentences or a list. Low priority.

## Other framing observations (not scored separately)

- **Framework paragraph length (line 121–137) — MAJOR 3.** Eighteen lines that pre-run §3 and
  §7 in full: all three gates with parenthetical definitions, the rhetorical "The gate that is
  easy to overlook is the market-access one" (line 126), a preview of the unit-value evidence,
  both within-country comparisons, and the institutional gate's "separate fact." This is where
  the residual over-claiming concentrates and it unbalances §1 toward theory. Compress to:
  name the three gates in two sentences; state "we argue the market-access and irreversibility
  gates are both adverse here (§§6–7), on within-country evidence we describe as suggestive
  rather than dispositive"; stop. The "easy to overlook" rhetorical setup can stay — it is the
  paper's genuine framing insight and §7 line 634 backs it ("close to sufficient").
- **"well-identified" (line 143)** is defensible for the *trade shock* after the surge-basket
  DiD rebuild (γ = 2.44, p = 0.013, wild-bootstrap 0.010, plus breaks + Armenia/Kyrgyz +
  placebo). It is the *null's attribution* that is not clean. The sentence structure — "a …
  well-identified … shock produced no detectable investment response" — lets "well-identified"
  bleed onto the null. Reword so the modifier stays on the shock.
- **QIC register (line 113–114):** §1 says the analysis is "supplemented by the transaction
  record of Kazakhstan's state investment corporation" as delivered fact; §3 line 335–338 has
  been softened to "public disclosure of the fund … the replication package pins the release
  date." Given the §7 "captive capital abstained" identification leans on this register and it
  was a round-1 hard gate before circulation, §1's breezy phrasing is slightly ahead of the
  data. §3/§10's job, not §1's — but worth a note since Step three previews it as in hand.
- **Neutral framing:** §1 contains no loaded language ("circumvention," "sanctions-busting"
  are absent; "corridor, not factory" is descriptive). Not flagged, per instruction — noting
  only that it is genuinely clean.
