## One-paragraph editor's assessment

The paper fits the special issue. It asks what an intermediary keeps from trade rerouted through a customs union, which is the issue's question. It also states its limits more honestly than most submissions we see. Both referees score it at about 60. Referee B finds the methods sound for what is now claimed. Referee A doubts the substance. That split usually means a competent paper whose framing has not yet earned its general lesson. The decision-relevant risk is Referee A's Concern 1: the investment null may be what the shock's nature predicts on its own (minimum efficient scale, sanctions exposure, and Russia as the natural production site inside the union). If so, the null is not a finding about customs unions. This can be fixed mostly by reframing, plus one cheap test that both referees point to. The other concerns are documentation, measurement and scope fixes. Several can be done in days. The path to publication is a shorter, reframed paper whose investment null is supported by official investment data.

## Referee summary

- **Referee A (domain, SKEPTIC):** 61/100 (59.6 by the weights, rounded up by the referee). Recommends Reject (borderline), but would support a Major Revision conditional on Concerns 1 and 3. The positive results are honestly bounded, but the null may be predicted by the nature of the shock, and Sections 10–12 recommend more than the evidence supports.
- **Referee B (methods, MEASUREMENT):** 59/100 (capped at 70 by one FAIL on deal-universe documentation). Recommends Major revision and finds no fatal concerns. The problems are documentation and measurement, not identification, and all can be fixed.

## Decision rule applied

- **FATAL:** 1 (C1), and it is addressable by reframing plus a cheap test.
- **ADDRESSABLE:** 8 (C2–C9).
- **Rule rows that apply:** "1 FATAL (addressable), any ADDRESSABLE → Major revision" and "0 FATAL, 4+ ADDRESSABLE → Major revision". Both give **Major revision**.

**How the rule resolves the referee split.** The scores are effectively tied: A's raw weighted score is 59.6 and B's is 59.4. The recommendations differ only in how each referee weighs C1. Referee A's Reject rests on C1 alone. A states that Concerns 3 and 4 can be fixed by cutting and Concerns 2 and 5 with available data, and that a Major Revision conditional on Concerns 1 and 3 is the minimum A could support. C1 has a credible fix through framing and one within-design test, so the rule gives Major revision, not Reject. This also matches the "methods OK, substance doubts" pattern: a technically competent paper that needs a framing ask.

## Where the referees disagreed

1. **Verdict (Reject-borderline vs Major).** Resolved above. Editor's view: Major, with C1 as the condition that decides the next round.
2. **Whether C1 is fatal.** A says closest to fatal; B says no fatal concerns. Editor's view: fatal **if unaddressed**, addressable in practice. The paper already concedes the comparative-advantage reading (§3, §8) and lists compliance exposure as a confound (§2, Limitations). What is missing is promoting compliance exposure from confound to framework element, and an ex-ante prediction.
3. **Which way the margin m is biased.** A argues m is too *low*: scarcity rents earned by Kazakh residents are retained value added. B argues the floor may be too *high*: freight is earned by foreign carriers, and an assembly comparator raises the ratio. Editor's view: the referees push in opposite directions, so the 6–14% band is not obviously biased one way. Report a two-sided band with both scenarios. The "about a tenth" reading holds up to m ≈ 0.12, and "about a third" holds even at the 34% scarcity case.
4. **Expand or shrink the framework.** A wants a fourth, legal gate added; B wants the framework cut back to an organising device. Editor's view: do both. Name the compliance/legal constraint, and strip the binding-constraint language.
5. **Investment data.** This is agreement more than disagreement: both want official fixed-capital investment by activity. A adds firm registrations; B adds the civilian-line divisions and the sector share of deals.

## Recommendation from the guest editors to the journal editor

We recommend **Major revision**. Two referees (domain/SKEPTIC 61, methods/MEASUREMENT 59) agree on the paper's strengths: fit with the issue's customs-union theme, unusually candid bounding, and transparent calibration. They split only on whether the investment null is a puzzle or what the sanctioned nature of the flow predicts. That objection can be answered within the paper's design, by reframing and by a test on the non-sanctioned civilian surge lines. The remaining concerns are measurement, documentation and scope. No reproducibility evidence was available to us either way. We would expect a second round to decide on whether C1 and C2 have been answered.

## Survival judgement (qualitative)

- **Guest editors' desk, as currently written:** good odds of being sent out. Fit with the theme is strong, the contribution can be stated in one sentence, and the candour reads well at the desk. The main desk risk is that the closest prior paper (Chupilkin, Javorcik and Plekhanov) is already in EER, so the "what we add" paragraph must be sharp.
- **External review, as currently written:** a reject is a live possibility, not a remote one. With two or three referees, the chance that at least one shares Referee A's view is substantial. Overreach in Sections 10–12 and the footnote 2 and Table 3 issues give such a referee easy material. A major revision is the most likely single outcome, but not by a wide margin.
- **After Tier 1 alone:** the odds of a revise decision improve clearly, to better than even. The skeptic's easy targets are gone, but C1 is answered only by argument.
- **After Tier 1 plus item 10/11:** the strongest version, with the investment null and the C1 response both backed by evidence. Eventual acceptance still faces a low-teens base rate. Given the special-issue fit, this paper would be well placed within that pool, but not a favourite.

These are judgements from two simulated reports, not estimates. A real panel could differ, especially on how much weight it gives the sanctions framing.
